Why real yields matter for gold — and why the link can break

A short lesson on opportunity cost and the limits of a single-factor explanation.

Published 25 Oct 2026, 23:41 IST · By FOXREX Desk · AI-assisted editorial

A real yield measures a bond’s return after allowing for inflation. Gold does not pay a coupon, so a higher inflation-adjusted return on competing assets can raise the opportunity cost of holding it.

That is a useful framework, not a mechanical trading rule. Research by the Chicago Fed examines real rates alongside inflation expectations and pessimism about future economic conditions. The World Gold Council also cautions that higher real rates are not automatically negative for gold when other demand forces are active.

A practical reading sequence
Check whether nominal yields and inflation expectations moved together. Then ask whether risk sentiment or demand for gold has changed. Finally, compare that explanation with observed prices rather than assuming the relationship must hold.

Educational example
If a nominal yield rises while expected inflation rises by more, the implied real yield can fall. Calling that simply “higher yields” misses the distinction. This example uses no current market figures and makes no prediction.
